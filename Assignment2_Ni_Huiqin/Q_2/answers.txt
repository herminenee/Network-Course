Homework 2 - Question 2
Comparison of Random Walk and Random Waypoint Mobility

Simulation settings:
Number of nodes: 100
Simulation duration: 400 seconds
Region: 80 m x 80 m
Random number seed: 123456789

Part 1: Random Walk and Random Waypoint Comparison

For speeds uniformly distributed between 4.0 and 6.0 m/s, the
calculated average distances from the center point (40, 40) were:

Random Walk:
Average distance from center = 30.1803 m

Random Waypoint with a 2-second pause:
Average distance from center = 24.7399 m

The Random Walk results show that the nodes are distributed relatively
uniformly throughout the 80 m x 80 m region. In this model, each node
changes its movement direction every 2 seconds, and the movement process
does not repeatedly choose destinations that favor paths through the
center. Therefore, the final node locations remain broadly distributed
over the square.

The Random Waypoint results show a greater concentration of nodes near
the center. Although the waypoints are selected uniformly throughout
the region, paths connecting pairs of random waypoints pass through the
central portion of the square more frequently than through its edges
and corners. This creates the well-known center-concentration effect of
the Random Waypoint model. Consequently, its average distance from the
center, 24.7399 m, is smaller than the Random Walk result of 30.1803 m.
The short 2-second pause is not long enough to eliminate this spatial
bias.

Part 2: Random Waypoint with Higher Speed and Longer Pause

For speeds uniformly distributed between 20.0 and 22.0 m/s and a
40-second pause time, the calculated result was:

Random Waypoint with a 40-second pause:
Average distance from center = 29.2550 m

This distribution is more spread out than the Random Waypoint result
from Part 1. At the higher speed, nodes require relatively little time
to travel between waypoints. They then remain stationary for 40 seconds
at waypoints that are selected uniformly over the square. Therefore,
the nodes spend most of the simulation time paused at uniformly selected
locations instead of moving along paths that frequently cross the center.

As a result, the average distance increased from 24.7399 m to 29.2550 m
and became closer to the Random Walk value of 30.1803 m. The remaining
difference can be attributed to the finite number of nodes, the particular
random realization, and the time that some nodes still spend traveling
between waypoints.
